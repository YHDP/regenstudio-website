'Ponte em Cena' is the combination of two very different fields of study. Isaac Medeiros is an actor and teacher in theatre and film; Yvo Hunink de Paiva is an innovator and designer. During a conversation, the realisation came that while the tools are different, their goals are often the same: empathy and connection.

What 'Ponte em Cena' is

'Ponte em Cena' puts those tools in the same room and treats them as an interwoven process.

The participatory-theatre tradition Boal developed in 1960s and 70s São Paulo, refined during his European exile in Lisbon and Paris after 1976, and turned explicitly legislative in 1990s Rio, where as a municipal vereador he used staged community scenes to draft real city law. At its core sits Boal's spect-actor: neither passive audience nor scripted performer, but someone who steps onto the stage to change what happens next. That figure is what we want to bring into the news-reading exercises that anchor the session.

To turn theatre into design and decision, we pair Boal's method with Dutch-origin schools of design, participatory consensus-based decision-making. Regen Studio has worked with these traditions for years — such as with energy communities — and they give the workshop an actionable layer to reflect on and off the stage.

Both are methods for thinking together in a room. 'Ponte em Cena' uses both, side by side, across a series of oficinas in 2026. The project's name — bridge in scene, in Portuguese — is meant exactly that way: a working bridge between two ways of getting to insight, not a piece of stagecraft about one.

The frame: 200 years of friendship NL ⇄ BR

The Netherlands and Brazil mark two centuries of diplomatic relations in 2026, and the Dutch government in Brazil is using that anniversary to fund cultural-exchange projects through the Cultural Connections 2026 call. That is why we are trying to design the workshops in the spirit of 200 years of friendship.

We will deliver three different formats of a workshop, each in a different context. We will attempt cultural exchange during a workshop setup with only one culture in the room; a workshop with two cultures in the room that have a common agenda; and a workshop with an important Dutch topic and a Brazilian public attendance, and therefore unpredictable participants and outcomes.

Why this fits Regen Studio

Most of what Regen Studio works on is system-level: traceability, Digital Product Passports, energy transitions, regulatory architecture. The line that runs through all of it is the same line that runs through 'Ponte em Cena': method matters, often more than the artefact. How a group of people gets from a vague shared problem to a shareable answer is itself a designable thing, and the better that method is, the better the outcomes downstream.

Cultural exchange is not where we usually do that work — but it is one of the few settings where a method-led project can really show what method does. We will write again when the oficinas are in motion.
