The half you can't yet quantify

Carbon accounting is settled. Tonnes of CO₂e, factor-versioned, range-bracketed, audit-traceable — the discipline is mature enough that an auditor knows what to ask for and a sustainability lead knows how to deliver it.

Biodiversity impact is the harder half. ESG, CSRD, CSDDD and TNFD frameworks increasingly ask for evidence — not narrative — that operations and supply chains don't degrade the ecosystems they sit in. For building-materials value chains, that question lands hard: forestry sourcing, quarry footprints, site-development impact, post-construction recovery. And most current biodiversity disclosures fall into one of two categories — static "is the site within X km of a Natura 2000 area?" or narrative "we work with local stakeholders to protect biodiversity."

Neither produces measured, longitudinal, site-specific evidence. Neither tells you whether the site is becoming more or less biodiverse over time.

Birds as a recognised proxy

Avian species richness has been used as an ecosystem-health indicator across decades of conservation biology. Birds occupy multiple trophic levels, respond rapidly to land-use change, and are observable without invasive sampling. Standard diversity indices — Shannon, species accumulation, IUCN-weighted richness — are well understood by ecologists and increasingly by sustainability auditors.

This isn't a novel methodology. It's a non-invasive, scalable application of established ecological monitoring.

How AviSonar works

AviSonar listens for birds continuously, on-device. It identifies species via BirdNET-Analyzer — Cornell Lab's open species models — applies IUCN Red List status across 249 species, and surfaces site-level analytics: heatmaps, accumulation curves, frequency calendars, Shannon diversity, species comparison.

Local-first by design: no account, no cloud, no data leaves the device. Crucially, AviSonar ships with extensive verification tooling — a human can confirm or reject each detection, turning raw audio recognition into validated, verifiable data. A laptop and a simple microphone become a bird sensor in minutes; planned Raspberry Pi support will let multiple sensors blanket large or hard-to-reach sites simultaneously.

Same discipline as your carbon numbers

AviSonar's records carry the same audit discipline the Regen CO₂ Footprint Manager applies to carbon:

  • Numbers come with confidence ranges, not false precision.
  • Calculations are reproducible — re-run them in three years and get the same answer.
  • Sites aggregate cleanly into portfolio totals.
  • The original audio sits attached to every record, so an auditor can verify any figure end-to-end.

The biodiversity-disclosure field itself — TNFD, CSRD ESRS-E4, SBTN, EU-Taxonomy "do no significant harm" — is still maturing, and assurance methodology is being built in real time. AviSonar doesn't settle which framework you follow. It gives you measured, longitudinal, site-level data with the traceability discipline assurance providers expect of any ESG figure — so when your framework and your auditor ask for evidence, you have it.

Where it lands in a building-materials value chain

  • Forestry and timber sourcing — baseline biodiversity at and near concession boundaries, year-on-year delta.
  • Quarries, aggregate and mining sites — pre-/during-/post-extraction monitoring.
  • Production sites — perimeter and surrounding-land biodiversity as part of site-level disclosure.
  • Construction-impact sites — pre-/post-construction comparison, recovery curve over years.
  • Reference sites — undisturbed control points for like-for-like comparison.
  • Effect of interventions — measure whether your planting, insect-hotel, or greenery-policy initiatives actually shift the species count over time.

In each case, AviSonar produces the measured trend that the ESG framework asks for, and the auditor can verify.
